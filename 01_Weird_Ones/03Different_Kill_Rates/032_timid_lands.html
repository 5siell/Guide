Timid Lands

5x3, neutrals 2/2, wastelands 15x3, base: +3 Map: https://imgur.com/MxvhxgT, 1x +1 reinforcement card each turn.

Pick either for FTBs, good combos or position. Or all 3. Step 1, Get army advantage. Step 2, convert that into an elimination or income.

Outline

Picking, Gameplay and Different Strategies

Separate the potential distributions into patterns/categories. +3 FTBs / +2 FTBs / No FTB.

Picking

  1. Just pick the +3 FTB. Or cover it at least.

    • Basically every +3 in the map is really strong due to a) fast income as well as b) position and ability to expand with the exception of "Dalmatians" (example), light blue +3 on the East. It’s sort of isolated and one-dimensional. You could argue against me saying "Singing Mermaid" on the West is even more isolated, but that one actually has faster access to the cluster of +3s/+2s through the island and an efficient great +4 to expand into.

    • When Not to pick the +3 FTB.

      • Generally speaking you need to convert the 3->6 Income T1 into something, either on T2 or T3. If not, then you at least want to be able to expand into your bonuses with 2 Turns. So think of it this way. If you pick the +3 FTB, the rest of your picks should be around playing aggressively towards an elimination/a combo/something. If you decide not to pick the FTB, you play for a prolonged, positional game.

      • There’s some very brawly distributions where either the FP or a combo provide safety, like here, the idea being 7-10 cover a great region, 2-6 the FTBs and 1 a single safe pick. Only contact is at 8-9, as such you have the time to get your safe bonus.

      • Here, Jacob’s FTBs translates to an elimination T2 but falls off on T4 onwards, where positionally Rufus threatens income north and Jacob doesn’t suspect the center.

    • Picking the +3 FTB can be as easy as 1/2/3 picking it. In a distribution with a +2 FTB on top you have to find the perfect configuration that gives you the upper hand regardless of move order. The latest picks you can cover a +3 with and 100% guarantee coverage is 2/4/5.

    • In 60/70 kill-rates covering such a configuration comes often with 2/4/5 +3 FTB & 1/3 +2 FTB and similar paths. Here, it depends on

      1. the position of each pick

      2. the strategy (e.g. having 2/3 picks in the +3 FTB and a contact T1 is good cause you threaten elimination). (e.g. you deliberately pick in a way that counters the expansion of the +3 FTB).

      3. ABBA, in 2 ways.

        1. Let’s say you pick 1/2/4 +3 FTB 3/5 +2 FTB, and receive back 2/3 and lose 1/4/5. You are have one pick vs two picks in the +3 FTB but first move on Turn 1 to not get eliminated since the attacker in reverse kill-rates has the advantage.

        2. Expand that logic. 5 picks of the board are forced. That means SP gets their 6/7. Play around getting a good combo on your 6/7 and finish off your coverage with 8/9/10, since you opt for three-four picks from your 1-7 and coverage afterwards.Example of 1/4/5 +3 FTB with 2/3 +2 FTB and different approaches after 5th. After first five picks, Moto’s compensation for SP is the FTB west with 8910 coverage. Rufus’ compensation is 6/7/8 combo that allows SP to go 3->3->9 if center doesn’t counter the +2 FTB. If it does, it’s a 7vs6 income plyable game as long as you out-delay T2. 6/7/9 for 9 pairing with 1st pick, 10 for 6/7/10 and counter to first pick’s expansion from 1/4.

    • If 2 +3 FTBs pick them both!.

  2. Picking the +2 FTB simplifies the stage of picking. Ultimately, picking it or not has to do with:

    • Are there neighboring territories you plan on picking -> FTB gives edge on T2/T3. Example

    • Is the board rich, with a lot of +3 FTBs and +3/+4 Combos that I cannot even fit a slow +2 FTB in my picks -> it’s ok to not pick it.

    • Does my +2 FTB have either decent coverage or expansion (be versatile) -> That’s relative to the distribution.

      • "Naked Willie Object" - Red +2 on the West is 99% of the time great.

      • The blue +2 "Turtle Bay" in the south is suspicious (40% WR in games above >1800 Rating)

      • "Gaussian Hill" Green +2 in the center as an FTB with blue +3 sure, other 2 options extremely situational to the board. For example here it would have to expand in a bonus around T3/T4 that by then it would be controlled by 2 regions richer in income and with better bonuses (west +4 and north +3).

      • "QuickMarch" light blue +2 center, "Artificial World Peace" pink +2 and "Ninja Fortress" +2 north usually fantastic.

  3. No FTB Distributions -> try not to panic. There’s a lot of ideas here around the opening combos below. In reality, all those distributions with just one +2 FTB fall in this category as long the players trade a pick there.

    • Are there good combos that elevate your gameplay, check Table 1 (usually combos are contested. Hard to find games of someone not contesting good combos).

    • Pick for position. Examples of that:

      1. Example 1, 2 great income regions in the west and north secured by triple picks, 7-10 to fill in the +2 FTB and +3 combo.

      2. Example 2, 4x pick Best region on the map (Center, NE lackluster)

      3. Example 3, purely positional triple pick in the north.

      4. Example 4, triple pick north and 4/5 south safe combos for better position & coverage afterwards.

      5. Example 5, having first pick is just awesome.

Gameplay

You need to know your kill-rates. And you need to know when not to attack. Yes I said that the attacker has a better ratio at 75/50 and attacking is always better than defending but there’s an asterisk here. For example here North, Crazy finds the only way to lose the game by wasting 2 OP cards T1 into wasting a deployment and a spawn T2. I’m attaching this Fast Earth game in this section as well, to illustrate the ridiculous advantage the attacker has. This is possible due to 75% kill-rates and a case size of how strong 2/6/5/10 size attacks (in this case 6).

On this topic, OP cards are disgustingly overpowerful in reverse kill-rates, play them to win the game/get bailed out a losing position. For example here on Turn 4 none of the players can back off and you fist-fight it 2x OP from both GO. But that is not always the case. If you waste both your OP cards and don’t win the game off that, your opponent is heavily favored because they can control the move order whenever they please. For example here Buns uses 2x OP on T3 and loses to consequent OP attacks on T6/T7. Different example , here Jacob uses 2x OP on T3 and Buns punishes with subsequent OP cards on T4/T5.

Beyond picking theory, combinations and ABBA, the game is all about army advantage converted into eliminations. The player ahead looks to punish with first moves. The player behind looks to play as tricky as possible making bold predictions. Then the attacker has to deviate and also predict the predict. Take note in applications of such tricks, as followed:

  • Example 1, Turn 4 tornado has priority, timi moves 2nd and has less income, chooses to let tornado over-commit and waste OP cards and captures back with 2+2+6 attacks for an army advantage out of thin air.

  • Example 2, Rak defends by attacking, baits 2x OP cards and recaptures back.

  • Example 3, 7vs7 income face off, Rufus finds the winning configuration through a recapture setting up a forced break with priority T7.

On Cards, the template features RF cards in increments of +1, every single turn (as long as you capture a territory). You can use them however you want, be it for expansion or for bigger stacks of armies; the versatility is quite nice. Ideally you’d want to maximize their output by forcing something, be it surviving & finishing an expansion bonus or big stack of armies that gets in the opponent’s position.
