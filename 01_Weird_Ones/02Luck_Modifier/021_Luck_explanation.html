Luck Modifier

How Luck works

https://www.warzone.com/wiki/Luck_Modifier

Welcome to the rabbit hole that is luck. Thankfully, the 2 templates in MTL that involve luck are simpler than this shit-show that is calculating the binomial probability of an attack succeeding. If you want to go in depth about how it works, go ahead. I will do my best in explaining it as simply as possible, which also linking to you the code in order to calculate it as fast as possible. Let’s Define:

  1. A→A \rightarrow Attacking Armies

  2. D→D \rightarrow Defending Armies

  3. L→L \rightarrow Luck Percent

3 Steps in our calculations. Baby steps:

  1. Binomial Probability P(x;p,n)=(nx)px(1−p)n−xP(x;p,n) = \begin{pmatrix}{} n \\ x \end{pmatrix} {p}^x(1-p)^{n-x} What happens behind the scenes when attacking in Warlight. Imagine attacking with 3 armies. That means 3 integers are randomly generated behind the scenes between 1 and 100 (RNG). for kill rate 60% or 0.6, then:

    • if RNG>60, successfull roll

    • if RNG<60, unsuccessfull roll

    That of course means there are 4 scenarios, 0,1,2 and 3 successful rolls. Or, mathematically, the number of successfully rolls, defined as (i), takes values from 0 to A. That’s where the binomial distribution comes in play. P={A!i!×(A−i)!×0.6i×(1−0.6)(A−i),for i=[0,1,...,A]P = \begin{cases} \frac{A!}{i! \times(A-i)!} \times 0.6^i \times (1-0.6)^{(A-i)}, & \text{for } i=[0,1,...,A] \end{cases} Following till now? Good.

  2. Armies killed K=Round[L×(i−(0.6×A))+(0.6×A)]K = \text{Round}[L\times(i-(0.6 \times A))+(0.6 \times A)] By round, I refer to rounding K to the closest integer

  3. Addition of the Probabilities For A attackers there are A+1 number of rolls. We spot which of these rolls correspond to killing equal or more than D, defined as the defending armies. Then sum up their corresponding probabilities

What does this mean? Short story it means Luck is a bad feature. Long story is some people think luck is a good feature. Those people either don’t know how luck works, or are serial gamblers. (jk, I’m being harsh about something trivial.) Luck barely affects gameplay 99% of the times. It makes expansion over a bonus without a starting territory in a bit more difficult and that’s usually all there is to it.

But Chris, do I really need to calculate all these when I am playing the game? No young Padawan. You can press Game on the bottom left →\rightarrow Analyse Attack, input the number of defending armies and see a graph of numbers of attacking armies with their corresponding probabilities of success in killing the specified number of defenders.

But Chris, you may ask, does the Luck setting only affect attacking and not defending? It should affect both right? How can I see how Luck affects the number of armies that defenders can kill based on Luck.

Well, it affects both attacking and defending obviously. And you cannot see it in the game somewhere in the graphs, haHA. Here, on Turn 7, look at Yelo’s 1st move of 26 armies capturing a neutral of 4 territories. By what we know, 4x0.7=2.8, which rounds to 3 armies. So, we expect to see 3 attacking armies die in this attack. However, only 2 attacking armies die!

By applying the same exact methodology as above, but now for a kill rate of 0.7 and accounting for the defending armies, we calculate that 4 defending armies have 100% chance of killing 2 attacking armies, and 99.19% chance of killing 3 attacking armies. The cumulative luck graph in-game does say that Yelo won 1 army in the process, which is the factual outcome of this. So in the end, despite how ridiculous 99.19% chance of an event failing seems, it doesn’t really matter.

By this point you have realised that Luck doesn’t really affect small attacks (10 or less attacking armies). In fact, for the attacker’s luck, it affects:

  • 4 on 3. an attack you would never do regardless unless you are a lunatic (12.96% to succeed).

  • 6 on 4. pain. Example. Turn 8 and 11 (82.08% chance to succeed).

  • 7 on 5. an attack you should never do regardless, unless you are a serial gambler and need to get into rehab (2.80% chance to succeed).

  • 8 on 5. pain. Example. Turn 2 (95.02% chance to succeed).

  • 9 on 6: an attack only someone drunk would make (23.18% chance to succeed)

As the stacks of armies increase, Luck is more likely to affect the outcome. For example, from Yelo vs Octane once again https://www.warzone.com/MultiPlayer?GameID=35445501, turn 11 there are attacks of 41 and 86 armies. 41 Attacking armies:

  • 2.74% chance to kill 26 defenders 0.0019% chance to kill 27 defenders
