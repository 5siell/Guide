%\textbf{Friday, June 13th, 2025}.
%I have a simple motto: be the person who creates the material others can learn from. Rather than wait for consensus—or complain when no one takes the initiative—I invest my own time to build the change I want to see. Over the past two years that impulse has drawn me into more Warzone projects than my free time could reasonably absorb, all fueled by genuine love for the game. In hindsight, I bit off more than I could chew.
\\
\\
%My "relationship" with Warzone began casually in 2016, after a university friend mentioned it in passing. For years it was just a puzzle against the AI. Most of my real "gaming" went into Pokemon and then Pokemon Go (PoGo) and Ingress.  I got to travel the world through them, host events in many ways and get fulfillment from interacting with a very active and passionate community (cannot state enough how passionate people that play Pokemon are). I remember moving to a different city in 2018 and finding friends there through Pokemon Go on July the 8th, where coincidentally a local had birthday so they had prepared a Squirtle-birthday cake. I'm reminiscent of that day.
\\
\\
%After relocating abroad and later in 2023, a pile of real-life stress turned Warzone from puzzle to refuge. I tried to break the cycle by deleting my first account and distancing myself from a particularly toxic sub-community—but by then the numbers, the scripts and the quest for improvement already had their hook in me.
\\
\\
%When the organizers of ML3 abandoned the event mid-way, I stepped in to finish it—ironically alongside some of the same people I had wanted to avoid. The deeper I became involved, the more obvious the pattern grew: whenever life felt overwhelming, I retreated into the game. Completing this document is therefore both a milestone and a farewell. I intended to keep helping within my own clan and among people I trust, but I was stepping away from the broader competitive scene.
\\
\\
%That decision changed how people speak to me. DMs arrived with apologies or belated empathy - sometimes from players who, hours earlier, treated me like a villain. Behind the banner of the "strategic community" I see insecurity and a need to justify endless hours spent chasing Elo. I no longer feel anger; only fatigue, and perhaps a little pity. My passion for the game has faded—not because of Fizzer’s stewardship, but because of the entitled minority that dominates the conversation.
\\
\\
%\textbf{Sunday, July 6th, 2025}.
%Two years of Warzone have shown me how starkly it differs from games I used to love. PoGo rewards friendliness; Warzone rewards min-maxing. In PoGo you meet a stranger in the park and lend a power-bank; in Warzone I know maybe five real first names after two years. PoGo offers many ways to enjoy the game; Warzone reduces everything to winning or losing. Small wonder newcomers drop out.
\\
\\
%The final push to quit the game came when a handful of trolls spent forty days parading fake ROI spreadsheets that priced past girlfriends like stocks, escalating to misogyny, racism, and even jokes about child exploitation. Half the server half-believed; the attention kept them going. When criticism came in, they threw back the "it's just a joke" card. When that didn't work out, they went for avoiding responsibility and deflecting the blame to me. "Chris roasted me daily, I had to play the idiot, planned this for months since an event 8 months ago, he deserved it". I deleted every profile. The MTL guide appeared then like mount Everest, impossible to get to the top. A couple of days later and after successfully forgetting most things due to real life priorities and rants, I failed a report, felt relief, and decided to finish this file with whatever material I can work with.
\\
\\
%I look back at that Squirtle Community Day, at when we brought Ingress First-Saturday events in Greece, at a Helsinki afternoon spent re-charging my phone at a stranger's apartment, and wonder where things went wrong. It you never played these games beyond July 2016, you couldn't understand situations like sitting in a coffee with an auto-catcher playing board games when suddenly a 100\% IV bagon spawns 20km away and you get in the back of a van of a stranger to get there. Let alone the weird ones, we also did car trips for taking down gyms or operations across not only cities but sometimes states and even countries. Yes countries. I went on a 300km roadtrip with 3 people I barely knew to hike 2000m and capture a portal at the border between Greece and Bulgaria. One night at a pub after a couple of beers we just decided to get in the car and drive to pozar public thermal baths at 1am 200km away from us just because we could and we accidentally found there and caught a perfect IV foongus! And the worst part? We all had to work the morning after! Yet clan league players want 18 days of banked time you privileged little snowflakes.  In contrast, two years in Warzone repaid me with manipulation, lies behind a screen and cold usernames. Perhaps I felt it more because I came from warmer, face-to-face communities. Perhaps I compare apples with oranges. Perhaps this game was never made for me. Perhaps I became more cynical as I grew up and trusted people less, but then I look at some behaviors and conclude that the Internet and very fake socializing it provides messes with our minds.
