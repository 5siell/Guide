Map Dynamics

Tor-ture kind of grows on you for it’s chaos. This has more to do with the Picking Logic than Map Dynamics, but acts as an introduction to the template. Comparing the templates in the RMO WR category, from Tables [wr_rmo_templates_basics] to [table:wr_rmo_cards_trimmed], we can identify at first glance that Tor-ture is by a wide margin the template with the most territories. For contrast,

  • Strat ME 131 terr, 23 bonuses, 15 efficient ones, 3 picks per player.

  • FF7 112 terr, 23 bonuses, 15 efficient ones, 3 picks per player.

  • Raj 147 terr, 31 bonuses, 4 efficient ones, 4 picks per player.

  • Tor-ture 168, 35 bonuses, 19 efficient ones, 4 picks per player.

So Tor-ture is and isn’t in a big and chaotic map, there are certain areas that are better than others by design. But it remains that it has a low amount of initial spawns combined with only 6 wastelands and lack of a blockade card, which give it a well-deserved reputation of being a very chaotic template without perfect picks. I find tor-ture and strat me the perfect templates to understand map dynamics and how they define a template, so decided to put the most work in these two.

In picks you generally want to cover everything under the mindset that "I’ll get safe income somewhere, contest somewhere else". I’ve denoted with arrows the entrances to regions to better pass the argument of relative safety while considering the bonuses WR above 1800 rating.

  1. The light green region is 11 territories for 9 income and very very well protected. Single borders only from everywhere except north which is very niche. But the downside is that the region requires setup of 2 or 3 picks minimum. On top of being defensively good, immediate double border towards west and yellow region.

  2. Yellow region is volatile. The settings 4x4 5 income have you try to pick ftbs/solo +4s/combo +4s and the brown +4 fits really well in how it expands fast into the +3 bonus. On top, great access to the north, but somewhat awful borders.

    • Yellow vs Orange taking off the equation the X2 bonus between them, even if you get in their region you have a long way to go, you really need to hold the chokepoint, but to hold the chokepoint you need more income and yellow is 7/10 income, while orange can exceed that.

    • That’s where the X2 orange +3 bonus comes in, in how it has access to the best +3, typically ftb, in orange region, to yellow region, and to light green region. It’s not the best bonus on the map but it can be a great late counter.

    • Yellow region vs light green region you just admit defeat typically. The +3 in the yellows is so much weaker than the +3 in the light green with that triple border between them. On top, envision that as the yellow player after you break, you need 2 more steps to get into the +6. The light green player immediately borders your +4 bonus. (This falls into the argument of why SEA and Indonesia are superior bonuses defensively).

  3. Orange region is amazing if you don’t let your opponent in. If you do it’s hard to eliminate them. Orange vs Pink you have a small edge usually due to a) double border on their +3 b) ability to break everything beyond that +3 fast, while c) pink player has to go through single borders just to break 1 bonus every 2 turns.

  4. Light blue region is really good. So good that a new player will instinctively triple pick there, as usually it offers at least 1 ftb. It has some niche counters from north and south, remains extremely good.

  5. Pink region is usually a dog-fight. Has a great combo +5, great FTBs, people will pick there since it’s nice and perfect, and it works similarly to the orange region in that you cannot afford to let an opponent in. If you do, you lose.

So while I could never tier bonuses and regions, the bonus WR represents it’s strength both defensively and offensively, something that you need to evaluate instinctively. The areas outside those regions are situational.

On the chaotic nature of this template, in a lot of distributions players will disagree at which are the best bonuses for good reason. You might want to go for 1/2 combo 3/4 combo 5 some safe pick 6/7/8 coverage and fail miserably getting a combination from your top 5 picks. This happens in every template, but more often in Tor-ture. You can sort of use this to your advantage by picking in a way that forces your opponent to get their 1-4 and be stuck in a region. It’s all a mindgame. To illustrate what I mean, here Buns sees an FTB in the SW with a direct counter and a late counter and simplifies the position going for 1/2 ftb, 3/4 safe picks in remote areas, 5/6/7/8 cover SW, assuming that he will lose one of the 1/2 combo, and failing by getting 1-4. Picking Logic

This map is such a mess that the mere act of trying to put structure into picks and influencing the meta, would give rise to sets that can universally work and directly counter the ideas written down in guides or discussed by players. So take everything with a grain of salt, there are no perfect picks.
A lot of distributions look like me vs bechaa and tornado vs timi. Take for example the latter, with the +3 ftbs circled in Figure 3. The hard part comes at identifying a pick-set that can cover a lot of different ideas or provide counters to different ftbs, and there are a lot of different ways to approach the discussion:

Ask yourself if you can avoid picking the ftbs, rephrasing the question to can you find other picks that can dominate a region outside of the ftbs? Or find picks that can be used to counter the ftb’s expansion? It’s all in the eye of the beholder. For example here Hergul picks the +3 island bonus hoping to counter Buns’ expansion on turn 3/4 but failing the timing. So my advice would be to experiment, think and pick outside the box, especially given that this is one of the templates where knowing your opponent’s playstyle can help tremendously.

Gameplay

Just want to attach this masterpiece of a -2.5 luck on Turn 1 by mod as the first note.

Jokes aside, I personally cannot for the life of me play this template without intel or some hints on where my opponent is, to guide me. I’ll go back to 2 games I’ve linked before of me against Falstaff and MoriOri vs Beren. Regardless of what is happening in whatever "x" front/region, you have to expand towards your opponent’s cluster, because if you don’t and they do, you will inevitably lose. It really is that simple. If MoriOri hadn’t expanded towards north from his west spawn as early as turn 4, he would actually be late to 1 turn to entering the north cluster Beren has to pivot to after the fight east. Of course, that takes different forms, don’t tunnel vision on wasting armies on neutrals.

Generally, you’ll find 2 types of Tor-ture games out there. Category 1 features the boring 4 different +3 ftb games that are decided by turn 4-7 really, and are about small advantages and capitalizing your opponent’s mistakes. Category 2 are 20-turn chaotic games of pivoting and flanking to the other side of the screen. There safe and flexible income matter in the same way it does in Strat ME and British Raj, as if you get attacked, you want to pivot elsewhere and a vital part of the game is having armies in useful territories.

For example, in this game between Hergul and Rufus, there is a surprising lack of ftbs - fast income. Using the zones from Figure 2:

  • Purple and Orange zones don’t have fast income, but they are great expansion zones.

  • Yellow zone has a safe +4 combo (2 picks) that can provide some flexibility. It’s decently close to the orange zone, getting the +3 bonus between them, and it can go west and border the pink zone in 3 steps.

  • Light Blue zone has an ftb, into another +3 and then it sort of stops, besides some expansion south. Expansion east and north is extremely slow.

The players use different approaches. Hergul embraces the brawl. 1/2 combo +4, fast income, 3/4 ftb, 5/6/7/8 a +3 ftb below that can check the 3/4 combo as well. Rufus goes for a slower set of 1/2/3 safe remote income in the Pinks and Oranges. His 2 if he loses 1 covers both regions. 4/5/6 to counter Light Blue/Light Green region + a potential FTB, 7/8 combo +4 west. The 2 players spent cumulatively 22 minutes on those picks. Before committing every turn you have to identify in some way what does my opponent try to achieve, who has the advantage, and what’s my game-plan, when is it ok to lose in a certain region so I can win elsewhere, etc. As Hergul you get an 18vs17 income position of defending your bonuses and Rufus trying to find a way in, and there are 2 main ideas. One, play extremely defensively and expand slowly to some safe +3 bonus west for example, and Two, identify that you are 18vs17 income up by 10 or so armies and convert that advantage through an elimination. Only when Rufus has no other choice but to pivot can you change the plans.

The above scenario has to do on one hand with picks and on the other hand with understanding a position instinctively. Hergul’s picks after getting 1-4 are about getting a fight asap and eliminating your opponent. If you let them breath, build a stack, dance around and enter your position, everything crumbles. Rufus’ picks give flexibility in different outcomes. Safe income scattered and coverage/counter picks. If between Turns 5-11 Hergul doesn’t punish him repetitively to translate the army advantage into a win, Rufus will get in his position and break half your bonuses. On Turn 12 after 1 elimination Rufus has to pivot so as to match the expansion. Overall, the core difference in the 2 sets and really 2 ideologies, is that 1 of them requires you to be extremely precise and punishing; a single mistake is a surrender, while the other one revolves around capitalizing your opponent’s mistakes and being versatile no matter which pickset you face.
