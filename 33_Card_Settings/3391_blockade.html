Blockade

Blockade appears in almost every single template. The idea behind the card generally speaking is to sacrifice a territory you own to a neutral blocking your opponent towards completing their goal. It happens at the very end of the turn.

A unique way to use this card is explained in this Forum, the so called timi-blockade. As explained in the Transfer-only section, a failed transfer-only move locks the armies in place. You can use that to blockade a territory in a safer way.

You can expand on this idea through Frog’s Tips, although I’ve personally not cross-checked the validity nor usefulness of this.

  • dd – defenders (after deployments)

  • aa – enemy attackers (after his max deployment that you expect). If he goes to a stack of 40, a=40, not 39, ignore 1-army stand guard here.

  • z1z_1 – neutral size you want if he does not attack for your blockade.

  • z2z_2 – neutral size you want if he does attack for your blockade.

  • ff – armies you issue as a transfer-only into the enemy territory (lock in place).

  • p∈[0,1]p\in[0,1] – fraction you pull back with a percentage transfer.

How to use, example game from the Forum:

  1. From TinyToad’s POV, you want to blockade Panama (in Central America).

  2. a is 40, his stack after max deploys. d is 31 cause we plan that.

  3. I choose to blockade with 7 if he doesn’t attack and 5 if he does attack.

  4. f=3.81f=3.81 round to 4 -> transfer only to the opponent

  5. p=0.91p=0.91 -> 91% transfer back last move

I haven’t seen this in action but play-tested a bit with it’s validity and it seems to work. For example in Turn 8, let’s say from sb’s perspective Marcus can do a max 11 attack in Scandinavia (a=12), and sb goes to d=12. In the game he does transfer-only 4 and blocks with 5. Well what if we test blockade of 5 if Marcus doesn’t attack and blockade of 3 if Marcus attacks (since his stack will be smaller and 3-> becomes 15 in this template). Inputting the number prints a transfer-only move of 1.3 -> rounds to 1 with a 72% move backwards which according to my drunk calculations works.

  • If Marcus doesn’t attack, it’s 12vs12, 11 movable for sb, transfer-only 1 forward. now 10 movable. 72% back that’s 7 armies transfered back. 12-7=5 -> block of 5.

  • If Marcus attacks with 11, 12vs12 becomes 4vs5 (sb on 5, 4 movable). Transfer-only 1 forward (now 3 movable), then 72% transfer back -> 2.16 -> 2. From his 5 armies after the trade, sb transfered back 2. 5-2=3 -> block of 3.

While it is mathematically correct, any mis-sizes you witness might occur simply from rounding.
