Move Order & Picking Phase

Explanation of Move Order - ABBA

r0.35

In accordance with Warzone Wiki Move Order Explanation, in a Warzone 1vs1 game between player A and player B, and under the assumption that player A has the first move, the move order alternates as ABBAABBAA…\text{ABBAABBAA}\dots. This sequence can be described as follows:

  • Player A executes their 1st1^{\text{st}} move.

  • Player B executes their 1st1^{\text{st}} and 2nd2^{\text{nd}} moves consecutively.

  • Player A executes their 2nd2^{\text{nd}} and 3rd3^{\text{rd}} moves consecutively.

  • Player B executes their 3rd3^{\text{rd}} and 4th4^{\text{th}} moves consecutively.

  • Player A executes their 4th4^{\text{th}} and 5th5^{\text{th}} moves consecutively.

  • Player B executes their 5th5^{\text{th}} and 6th6^{\text{th}} moves consecutively.

  • And so on, following the ABBA\text{ABBA} pattern, with an example at Figure [ABBA_image].

This pattern ensures that player A and player B alternate moves, with each player receiving consecutive moves in pairs after the first move by player A. The sequence ABBA\text{ABBA} then continues cyclically.

Of course, in the opposite case that player B has first move, then the move order follows the inverse pattern BAABBAABB…\text{BAABBAABB} \dots. The ABBA algorithm determines not only the order of moves but also the order of deployments, without exception. The arising question regarding the above rule, how is it decided which player moves first each turn. The answer to that question relies on the 3 different settings of implementation one can find upon creating a game. Needless to say, each game follows exactly 1 of the settings below:

  1. Random Move Order [random_move_order]

  2. Cyclic Move Order [cyclic_move_order]

  3. No-Luck Cycle Move Order [noluck_cycle]
